# Business Insights

## Executive Summary

The analysis covers 1,067,371 ecommerce transaction records from December 2009 to December 2011. After removing missing customer identifiers, cancellations, non-positive quantities and prices, and duplicate transaction lines, 779,425 valid records remained.

The final customer-level dataset contains 5,878 customers. The analysis shows a high overall repeat purchase rate, but also a significant decline in customer retention immediately after the acquisition month.

## Key Findings

### 1. Most customers made repeat purchases

- Total customers: 5,878
- Repeat customers: 4,255
- One-time customers: 1,623
- Repeat purchase rate: 72.39%

Almost three out of four customers placed at least two orders. This indicates that repeat purchasing was common among identified customers.

### 2. The second purchase took approximately three months

The average time between the first and second purchase was 97.52 days.

This suggests that customer engagement campaigns should remain active for at least three months after the initial purchase.

### 3. The largest retention decline occurred after acquisition

Weighted retention decreased from 100% in the acquisition month to 23.15% in month 1.

This represents a decline of 76.85 percentage points and is the most significant drop in the customer lifecycle.

### 4. Retention stabilized after the first month

Weighted retention remained approximately between 19% and 25% during most of the following observed months.

Month-3 retention reached 24.51%, showing that some customers returned after skipping one or more months.

### 5. Retention performance varied significantly between cohorts

The highest month-1 retention rates included:

| Cohort | Cohort Size | Month-1 Retention |
|---|---:|---:|
| 2009-12 | 955 | 35.29% |
| 2011-10 | 221 | 32.13% |
| 2011-08 | 106 | 27.36% |
| 2011-09 | 189 | 26.98% |
| 2010-10 | 377 | 25.73% |

The 2010-12 cohort had the lowest complete month-1 retention rate at 9.21%.

### 6. The oldest cohort generated substantial long-term value

The 2009-12 cohort generated approximately £5,316.65 in cumulative revenue per acquired customer during its first 12 months.

This demonstrates that retained customers can continue generating significant revenue long after their first purchase.

## Business Recommendations

1. Focus retention activity on the first 30 days after acquisition, where the largest customer decline occurs.
2. Continue follow-up communication for at least 90 days because the average second purchase occurs after 97.52 days.
3. Analyze the product mix, customer segments, and acquisition conditions of the strongest cohorts.
4. Investigate cohorts with unusually low month-1 retention and identify differences in customer or order behavior.
5. Create targeted campaigns for one-time customers to encourage a second purchase.
6. Track both retention and cumulative revenue because customer activity and financial value do not always follow the same pattern.

## Analytical Notes

- The analysis includes only transactions with a valid customer identifier.
- Cancelled orders and non-positive quantities or prices were excluded.
- The final month of the dataset was incomplete and was excluded from complete-period comparisons.
- Later cohort-index months contain fewer observable cohorts, so long-term retention comparisons should be interpreted carefully.
- The findings show associations in historical transaction data and do not establish causal relationships.
